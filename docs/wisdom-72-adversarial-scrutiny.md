# Wisdom Situations — Christian ethical red-team audit (72/72)

**Status:** Draft review on PR #1551. This is an editorial scrutiny ledger, not independent pastoral, legal, clinical, safeguarding or end-user validation. Do not merge as complete or describe the situations as psychologically proven difficult.

## Why the prior design failed

The most common failure was an option with obvious Christian compassion or honesty against three obvious forms of selfishness, negligence, control or concealment. A cost written as *you may be blamed* is not remotely equivalent to a dependent losing housing. Neither equal text length nor one-positive/two-negative labels proves true moral tension. The revision replaces 84 vague personal losses, adds 288 fact-specific grounds, replaces several unsafe/deceptive alternatives, and withholds any visible 'correct' answer.

## Eight adversarial checks for every question

1. **Four affirmative duties:** a good-faith Christian can state what legitimate obligation each option protects without defending deception, illegal conduct, abuse, or avoidable safety violations.
2. **Factual entitlement:** the positive case for each option is supported by an explicitly available person, policy, deadline, consent, right, or resource; hypothetical miracles fail.
3. **Two distinct harms:** one external and one decision-maker consequence, both concrete; guilt, criticism, shame, blame, inconvenience and generic reputation alone are insufficient.
4. **Sacrifice symmetry:** the apparently most generous option is not cost-free, and the seemingly restrictive option protects an equally real entrusted obligation.
5. **No easy workaround:** alternative appointments, anonymous disclosure, temporary housing, oversight and consultation are genuine options only where the case facts establish them.
6. **Non-negotiable boundaries:** honesty, consent, safeguarding, law, professional duties and immediate physical safety cannot be presented as morally disposable merely to produce four letters.
7. **No concealed answer cues:** option length, syntax, religious phrasing, visible compromise and placement cannot reveal an editorial key.
8. **Consequential defense:** the participant should be able to explain their choice to each affected person, acknowledge foreseeable harm, state what new fact would change the judgment, and defend at least one rejected option.

**Risk tags below indicate the need for additional domain review, not a psychological difficulty score.** 'Critical external review' cases involve safety, privacy, professional/legal duties, a particularly vulnerable party, or a risk of misleading doctrine. Nothing here is independently certified.

## Release gate

- Obtain domain review of all 28 high-risk cases, including safeguarding/legal/medical expertise where applicable.
- Have multiple mature Christian readers independently choose, defend and rank all four options without an author-provided answer; identify choices dismissed in under 10 seconds and why.
- Rewrite any option no reasonable reader can defend after being shown its case-specific facts; never force moral equivalence for genuine wrongdoing.
- Review the passages cited with their literary and historical context; verses are not mechanical answer keys.
- Test the full authored English version on phone-size layouts, including accessibility and screen reading.
- Tagalog and Cebuano retain the old translation source. The app falls back to current English until separately reviewed full-content translations exist. Full language parity and a proper locale indicator remain release blockers.
- Run unit, browser and CI smoke tests. Structural checks alone do not verify coherence or difficulty.
