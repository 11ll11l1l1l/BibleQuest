## Source-grounded visual rationale

**Angry-01 — PASS scene, HOLD selection.** The actual image shows a woman in a kitchen after conflict, clenched fist held at her waist while she looks down rather than shouting or striking; another adult faces away. The visible pause under tension is a contemporary alternative illustrating restraint in James 1:19–20 and Ephesians 4:26–32. This does **not** prove listening or reconciliation, and must not be represented as such. The guide's sports-shed towel scene is preferred but not the sole allowed action under the current contract. PR #1489 depicts that different sports-shed scene and already received scene-only PASS; two independent CLEANs cannot both be the canonical production master. This review neither supersedes #1489 nor changes #1438's other holds. Original square and portrait framing retain her fist; a center-cropped 16:9 hero likely removes the fist and is not scene-safe.

**Guilty — PASS scene.** A woman kneels to collect visible shards of a broken mug in a lived-in home. She faces consequences instead of concealing the mess, a concrete image of responsibility rather than generic sadness. 1 John 1:8–10 concerns acknowledging and confessing sin and God's forgiveness; the picture does **not** depict a confession or guarantee forgiveness for merely cleaning a cup. Romans 8:1 concerns those in Christ, not universal exemption from consequences. Square and centered portrait retain the face, hands and mug; a wide center hero crop cuts the shards at the bottom. Its broken-ceramic motif is distinct in action, location, and people count from the collaborative repair in Connected.

**Discouraged — PASS scene, uniqueness HOLD.** A man slumps at a desk with hand in hair, crumpled drafts and unfinished papers. The drafts establish repeated effort and discouragement, matching the retained canonical scene; Galatians 6:9 addresses not giving up in doing good, without promising that every desk task will succeed. Joshua 1:9 is spoken in God's commission to Joshua, not a general promise of task completion. Comparison with the live Anxiety CLEAN showed a notably similar dark-haired male in a dark top, hand on head, warm domestic interior, side-lit window, and downward gaze. This is **not** declared unique. Square and portrait retain face and drafts; wide hero crops lose lower papers.

**Excited — PASS scene.** A woman opens an unlettered sheet and visibly reacts with delighted surprise while a friend shares her happiness outside a modern building. This is an observable, differentiated action, not just a smiling portrait. Psalm 126:1–6 specifically celebrates God's restoration of Zion and prays for continuing restoration; the art is a relatable analogy for joy at good news, **not** a claim that an unknown letter is that historical deliverance. Romans 12:11 speaks to fervent service of the Lord, not excitement as an end in itself. Square retains letter and both faces; portrait likely retains the second face narrowly; crop QA still pending.

**Connected — PASS scene.** An older man and younger woman carefully hold and repair one cracked ceramic bowl together, smiling and coordinating their hands. This is observable mutual care and shared service, compatible with John 13:34–35 and 1 John 4:7–12, without asserting the scene depicts Jesus or proves Christian identity. It is not a guaranteed relational cure. Square and portrait keep both people and the bowl; center-wide crop risks the lower hands, so any hero usage needs separate focal proof. Ceramic motif recurrence needs the independent uniqueness role.
